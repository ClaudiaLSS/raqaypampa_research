## Methodological basis
 
No outdoor luminaire VA is modeled: every interviewed household places both LEDs indoors (*"un foco en
una habitación y otro está en la cocina"* — Félix; *"Tengo dos focos, uno en cada habitación"* —
Calixto), and no respondent mentions an exterior, patio, or latrine light. The overnight safety-light
practice is real and prevalent — *"a veces hago amanecer"* (Pánfilo); *"toda la noche la utilizo"*
(Santos); *"se queda prendido hasta que amanezca"* (Remigio) — with survey code 13 (18:00–05:00) in
3/11 households (52, 84, 86), and is modeled as a **single overnight lamp** (LED_1 only, `occasional_use =
0.29`) — revised 2026-07-28: an intermediate revision split this across both bulbs, but §1's household
structure (no dependents in residence, one sleeping room) makes two lamps overnight implausible, and no
interview quote describes more than one light; see VA4/VA8 — not treated as negligible.
No respondent states a cause (fear of fauna, companionship, or otherwise) for this practice; it is
handled as household-level heterogeneity with no stated cause, per protocol. The radio is broken,
missing, or unused in 7 of 9 interviewed households, so residual radio charging is folded into the USB
VA rather than modeled as a standalone load. Weather suppresses rather than drives load here — *"cuando
se nubla no carga… procuramos no cargar mi celular… eso es como el modo de ahorrar"* (Benancio) — the
SHS goes down under cloud cover, it does not create additional demand. These households are
predominantly agricultural (`occupation = Agriculture` for 10 of 11), which is precisely why there is
no daytime indoor load: the household is simply not home. Seasonal visit-driven uplifts are not
modeled, since visit frequency was never collected as a study variable and respondents contradict a
harvest-season lock (*"vienen en cualquier momento"* — Benancio; *"dos o una vez al año"* — Pánfilo);
per the protocol's retired conditional-VA mechanism, unmeasured triggers are Chaos, not a weighted
seasonal variant. `func_time` throughout is derived via `window_width × (1 − random_var_w)` (Protocol
§4) or, where no survey duration field exists, from an explicit interview duration statement, with the
fallback flagged. VA9 (charging) carries `thermal_P_var = 0.2` **[DECLARED DEFAULT]** per Protocol §9,
with the physical mechanism (CC-CV taper) stated explicitly.

## 1. Demographic summary
 
Households defined structurally by the **absence of school-aged dependents and of a working-age support
generation in residence** — unipersonal households (4/11) and older couples living alone (7/11) —
whose adult children have out-migrated to Cochabamba, Chapare, Santa Cruz or the mines.
7 of 11 households contain at least one member aged 60+; the household-head age range is 34–87
(mean 58.9, median 57).
 
> **Naming caveat, worth carrying into the paper.** "Isolated Elderly" is a *sociological* label, not an
> age bracket: the classifier keys on family type + no children in school, so it admits four heads under 50
> (ids 84, 48, 14, 52) whose isolation is structural (siblings gone, no dependents) rather than
> geriatric. Remigio Rodríguez (34) stays only because *"[no] puedo dejar aquí a mi mamá sola.
> ¿Quién va a sembrar y cosechar?"* — the profile is generated by out-migration, and he is the
> residue of it, not an elder. Either state this explicitly or rename the profile something like
> *"Post-migration Residual Households."*
 
These households are **agricultural** (10/11), work the fields on a normal 09:00–17:00 pattern, are
**sedentary year-round** (no temporary migration to the *monte*), and are sustained by intermittent,
unscheduled visits from migrated kin. Their energy signature is therefore **not** a lower-intensity copy of
Profile 1: it is a *differently shaped* load — a compressed, hedged morning, an early-terminating evening,
and a distinctive minority practice of leaving a bulb on until dawn.

## 2. The driving social rules (re-anchored to P2 evidence only)
 
- **R-A — The Sedentary Anchor (Structural: continuous 365-day occupancy).** Mobility is exhausted or
  unnecessary; the household never empties. *"Yo solo vivo aquí, aquí me mantengo… ya no puedo viajar ni
  moverme"* (Pánfilo, 25/03/26); *"Yo vivo aquí permanentemente, no voy a ningún lado"* (Santos, 26/02/26);
  *"de aquí nosotros no vamos al monte"* (Remigio, 20/11/24).
  **Model consequence:** the household-level structural-absence mechanism is **not** applied to Profile 2 —
  no VA suppression, no occupancy gap. Note that `migration_label = Yes` for ids 11, 14, 37, 52, which the
  interviews show refers to *the children who left*, not to the household. This is a concrete instance of the
  protocol's warning that `migration_label` cannot discriminate structural absence.
  **Corroborated by field memo across all nine interviewed households** (Protocol §2.1: Andrés 4/caseid 18,
  Benancio 11/caseid 29, Calixto 14/caseid 10, Pánfilo 37/caseid 49, Félix 48/caseid 37, Santos 52/caseid 39,
  Germán 57/caseid 32, Remigio 84/caseid 25, Valeriano 91/caseid 7) — each memo independently describes a
  sedentary head with adult children out-migrated to Cochabamba, Chapare, Santa Cruz, or the mines,
  consistent with the transcript-based R-A pattern rather than contradicting it. One memo adds a detail
  outside any transcript, noted here for completeness rather than folded into a parameter: Valeriano's
  house (91) reportedly serves as a church four nights a week (caseid 7) — a possible additional evening-use
  driver not otherwise evidenced; flagged in Open Items rather than built into VA3 without a timing anchor.
- **R-B — "Hacer amanecer": set-and-forget overnight illumination, with no stated cause.** A stable
  minority leaves a bulb burning until dawn. *"Yo la apago cuando ya está por amanecer… a veces hago
  amanecer"* (Pánfilo); *"toda la noche la utilizo; a veces la apago"* (Santos); *"Si no estamos aquí,
  prendido lo dejamos la luz, y así amanece"* (Remigio). **Crucially, none of them says why.** Remigio even
  leaves it on when *absent*, which rules out both fear-of-fauna and companionship as explanations.
  **Model consequence:** this is the protocol's *household-level behavioural heterogeneity (no stated cause)*
  pattern — a stable between-household difference, modelled as a flat `occasional_use`, adequate only under
  the profile-averaged Tier-2 validation design actually in use.
- **R-C — Capacity-driven demand suppression.** Age suppresses load through the body, not through the
  budget. *"estando mayores ya tenemos flojera… casi no ocupamos en las madrugadas"* (Benedicta / Germán,
  25/03/26); *"nuestras vistas ya no ven tan claro"* (id.); *"A veces estoy escuchando menos"* (Pánfilo);
  Valeriano is functionally blind (*"aunque no pueda ver ya bien, lo hice"*).
  **Model consequence:** invokes the protocol's **capacity-driven exception** in Table A — the *resulting*
  frequency is binned, with cause carried in the narrative. It suppresses (i) the morning light, (ii) all
  evening task lighting (weaving, reading), and (iii) the radio entirely.
- **R-D — Conservation under supply scarcity (cloud *reduces* load).** *"cuando se nubla no carga… en esa
  temporada nosotros procuramos no cargar mi celular… eso es como el modo de ahorrar"* (Benancio, 25/03/26);
  *"hasta dos o tres días deja de funcionar"* (Calixto). **Model consequence:** no weather-driven *uplift*
  anywhere in this profile, and a hard conservation-logic veto on any daytime lighting VA (protocol,
  Structural Requirement 3).
- **R-E — The Connectivity Lifeline (the one non-negotiable load).** The phone is the priority draw and the
  reason the system matters. Calixto, who owns nothing else: *"lo único que nos beneficia es para cargar el
  celular y la luz"*; asked what the SHS is for: *"Generalmente para comunicarnos."* Visits are real but
  unschedulable: *"Ellos vienen en cualquier momento"* (Benancio); *"dos o una vez al año"* (Pánfilo).

**No outdoor luminaire.** Also note the *single-active-bulb* pattern: 4 of 11 households effectively run one
bulb only (*"aunque tenemos dos, solo uno ocupamos"* — Benedicta; *"está alumbrando uno solo"* — Pánfilo;
*"un foco nomás está por el momento activo"* — Santos).

**Revised 2026-07-28 — morning and evening now modeled as one light, not two.** With only one or two
occupants and no dependents in residence (§1), a household running both LED_1 and LED_2 simultaneously in
the morning or evening window is implausible — a lone or paired elder occupies one room at a time. RAMP has
no mechanism to make two independently-drawn appliances mutually exclusive (`coincidence` in
`ramp/core/core.py` only governs multiple *units* of the *same* appliance), so rather than accept the small
but physically-impossible chance of both bulbs lighting at once, VA1 (morning) and VA3 (evening) are now
each modeled as **"whichever bulb is in use"** — a single VA at LED_1's wattage, with `occasional_use` set to
the *union* of the two bulbs' formerly-independent probabilities (`P(A)+P(B)−P(A)×P(B)`), not their sum.
VA5 and VA7 (LED_2's former morning/evening VAs) are consequently retired to structural placeholders, the
same treatment VA6/VA8 already had — see VA1/VA3/VA5/VA7 below. Net effect: LED_2 no longer contributes real
energy anywhere in this profile; its ~2 W is folded into the single merged light's LED_1 wattage instead of
being modeled as a second, coincident draw.

## 4. Daily social practices and anthropological windows
 
*These are the socio-temporal envelopes within which RAMP may place events — not periods of continuous
consumption.*
 
> **"Modal code," defined once.** The survey does not record clock times for lighting; it records a
> **categorical period code**. `light_1_morning`, for example, offers 13 pre-set windows — code 1 = 03:00–06:00,
> code 6 = 05:00–06:00, code 11 = 06:00–07:00, and so on — and each respondent picks exactly one. The
> **modal code** is simply *the code most respondents chose*, taken whole, bounds and all.
>
> Protocol §3 requires using the mode rather than any summary statistic computed across respondents,
> because averaging or taking a median of the *widths* (e.g. "the median window is 3 hours long, centred on
> a typical 18:00 start") manufactures a window **no respondent actually reported**. That error was caught
> three times in Profile 1. Reading the mode directly guarantees the window is a real answer somebody gave.
>
> The corollary matters here: where the codes are **bimodal** — two different answers tied for most common,
> as in Window 1 below — there *is* no modal code, and the honest response is to say so and widen
> `random_var_w`, not to split the difference and invent a window nobody chose.
 
### Window 1 — Hedged pre-dawn / early morning (04:00 – 07:00) → `[240, 420]`
Waking is early (survey `wakeup_time_after` mode = **05:00**) but *lighting* the wake-up is optional and
frequently skipped for reasons of physical capacity. Interview: *"En las madrugadas encendemos las luces
desde las 4:00 de la mañana… generalmente hasta las 7:00 a.m."* (Benancio); *"Nos levantamos a partir de las
4 de la mañana"* (Calixto); *"En la mañana a veces encendemos y a veces no"* (Andrés); *"Aproximadamente de
6 a 7 a. m."* (Benedicta).
**Survey grounding (modal-code check, protocol §3):** `light_1_morning` is *bimodal* — 05:00–06:00 (n=2) and
06:00–07:00 (n=2); `light_2_morning` mode = **06:00–07:00** (n=3). No single dominant code exists.
Window taken as the union of the interview-attested start (04:00) and the modal codes → **04:00–07:00**,
with the absence of a mode carried forward as high `random_var_w` rather than hidden by a false precision.
*Practices: rising, lighting the fogón, navigating the room. No school run, no communal shift.*
 
### Window 2 — Field day (07:00 – 18:00) → `[420, 1080]`
**No behavioural lighting load.** The household is *outside*: *"salimos a las 9:00 de la mañana y volvemos a
las 5:00 de la tarde"* (Calixto). Daylight is sufficient, the conservation orientation is strong (R-D), and
the one respondent who does close daytime visual work uses a **flashlight**, not the SHS bulb: *"a veces uso
la linterna en tiempos de cosecha"* (Calixto). This window exists in the model **only** to satisfy the
window-continuity requirement for the LED hardware — see VA2's `status: placeholder_artifact` note below.
 
### Window 3 — Evening: dusk to early sleep (18:00 – 22:00) → `[1080, 1320]`
The profile's principal load. Lighting starts at sunset and ends early: survey `sleep_time_after` mode =
**20:00** (5 of 7 reporting households sleep at 20:00–21:00). *"Aquí prendemos cuando el sol se pone,
aproximadamente desde las 6:00 o 7:00 de la noche"* (Andrés); *"desde las siete de la noche algo así, hasta
las nueve"* (Benedicta); *"A las 8:00 o a las 9:00, depende"* (Félix).
**Survey grounding:** `light_2_night` modal code = **18:00–22:00** (n=3); `light_1_night` is three-way tied
(18–22, 18–20, 18–05) but with an 18:00 start in 8 of 10 reporting households. Window = **18:00–22:00**,
the true modal code, *not* a median-of-widths construct.
*Practices: cooking, sharing dinner, conversation. Explicitly **not** task work — see R-C.*
 
### Window 4 — Overnight "hacer amanecer" (22:00 – 05:00) → `[1320, 1440] ∪ [0, 300]`
A **minority but stable** practice: the bulb is left on until dawn. Not a psychological or security
rationale — no rationale is offered at all (R-B). Where it occurs, it is a solid, continuous block.
*Practice: none. This is a passive background load, not an activity.*

## 6. Seasonality
 
The four-season agricultural calendar (planting Oct–Jan; growing/early harvest Feb–Apr; harvest May–Jun;
free grazing & migration Jul–Sep) is retained as **context**, but its energetic consequences for Profile 2
are worth stating precisely, and are mostly *negative*:
 
1. **These households do farm** (10/11), so the earlier claim that "they do not participate in farming
   activities" is withdrawn. What they do *not* do is **migrate** — R-A. During Jul–Sep, when Profile 1 and
   Profile 4 households empty out to the *monte* or to Chapare, **Profile 2 households stay put**
   (*"de aquí nosotros no vamos al monte"* — Remigio). Profile 2 is therefore the profile whose seasonal
   curve is **flattest**, and its distinctiveness in Jul–Sep is *relative*: it holds a baseline while others
   drop.
2. **No *evidenced* seasonal VA variant exists.** Kin visits — the one plausible seasonal uplift — were
   never collected as a study variable, and the interview evidence actively resists a harvest lock
   (*"vienen en cualquier momento"*, *"dos o una vez al año"*). Under the protocol's retirement of the
   conditional-VA mechanism, an unmeasured trigger is treated as **Chaos**, and its variance is already
   carried in VA9's `t_f_r_v = 0.30` and VA1/VA3's `random_var_w = 0.35`. §5's VA9 nonetheless carries a
   **provisional** Feb–Jun `func_time` override (240→420 min), added for full-year visualization
   purposes and explicitly flagged there as a plausible mechanism rather than a re-derived finding — this
   does not change the position stated here, that no seasonal VA variant is evidentially justified for
   Profile 2 from this profile's own data.
3. **The only defensible seasonal mechanism runs through *supply*, not demand** — the rainy season
   (Dec–Mar) suppresses generation for 2–3 days at a time (Calixto), which is already reflected in VA3's
   `occasional_use = 0.90`. If seasonality is to be modelled explicitly for this profile at all, it should
   be modelled on the **generation** side, not by editing behavioural parameters.
