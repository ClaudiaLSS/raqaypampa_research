### S5.6 Data Logger Installation

**Supplementary Material — Measurement Equipment**

**Data-logging hardware**

Continuous electricity telemetry was collected from the monitored Solar Home Systems (SHS) using two types of data logger. Both units measured DC voltage and current on the generation side (the photovoltaic module) and on the consumption side (the loads), from which instantaneous power and cumulative energy were derived.

**Deployment history and field conditions**

Two successive batches of equipment were deployed. An initial batch of **10 units of the older data-logger type** (originally built for a different project; see §S1.2) was installed first. Recurrent technical faults affected many of these units, and the entire older batch was ultimately withdrawn. A second batch of **8 units of the newer commercial type** (§S1.3) was then installed, some of which also developed faults over time.

Across both deployments, usable records were obtained from **14 households**. Per-household measurement coverage was uneven, ranging from about **one month to roughly one year**, reflecting the equipment failures described above and the difficulty of servicing units in the field.

Field conditions were demanding and materially affected data collection. Households are highly dispersed and geographically isolated, and the environment is very dusty; dust ingress and soiling contributed to sensor and hardware faults and to the variability in operational lifetime across units. Critically, the region lacks telecommunications connectivity, so **remote data retrieval was not feasible in practice**, despite both logger types advertising remote-transmission features (see below). Data were therefore downloaded on site from each unit's local storage during periodic field visits, which further constrained how continuously any given household could be covered.

**Older data logger (purpose-built for pico-PV systems)**

The first type is a data logger built for pico photovoltaic systems, originally developed for a separate project rather than the present study. Because these units were re-used from that earlier project, they were reconditioned before redeployment. Their input ranges and measurement resolution are matched to the low voltages and currents of a small SHS.

Key characteristics:

- **Maximum input (all channels):** 30 V, 5 A.
- **Monitored variables:** PV voltage, battery voltage, USB voltage; PV current, Jack-load current, USB-load current, and the logger's own consumption current; plus module open-circuit voltage (V_oc, used to estimate cell temperature) and short-circuit current (I_sc, used to estimate irradiance).
- **Inputs:** 1 × PV input (5.5/2.5 mm jack), 4 × load inputs (4.5/2 mm jack), 2 × USB inputs.
- **Current measurement:** 1%-precision shunts; 18-bit ADC with 0.3 mA resolution.
- **Voltage measurement:** 10-bit ADC; ±53.9 mV (0.4%) on battery and PV channels, ±29.9 mV (0.6%) on USB channels.
- **Overall measurement accuracy:** < 0.5%.
- **Sampling and aggregation:** all variables sampled every 5 s and stored as 10-minute averages; V_oc and I_sc sampled once per minute and likewise stored as 10-minute averages.
- **Storage and transmission:** local SD storage sufficient for one year; 10-minute averages nominally transmitted over GSM (not usable at the study site — see §S1.1).
- **Self-consumption:** 3.6 Wh per night (direct from battery); 7.2 Wh per day total.

**S1.3 Newer commercial data logger (Tycon TPDIN-Monitor-WEB3, adapted)**

The second type is a commercial remote monitoring and control device (Tycon Power TPDIN-Monitor-WEB3). It was originally designed for larger power systems, so its native measurement ranges are considerably wider than a small SHS requires; it was therefore adapted for the SHS scale of this study. [Describe the specific modification applied — e.g., use of an external current shunt on the dedicated shunt channel to recover resolution at low SHS currents, and/or the channel configuration used.]

Key characteristics (stock specification):

- **Voltage measurement (DC):** four channels, 0–80 VDC, accuracy ±0.1 V.
- **Current measurement (DC):** four channels — two bidirectional 0.1–15 A channels (20 A peak), one ±10 A channel, and one channel accepting an external shunt (±25 mV input); accuracy ±0.1 A. Bidirectional sensing distinguishes battery charging from discharging.
- **Temperature measurement:** two channels, −40 to 125 °C, accuracy ±1 °C.
- **Data logger:** FIFO, up to 4048 data sets, programmable logging interval.
- **Access and transmission:** web interface and SNMP with on-board history graphs and email alerts (remote access not usable at the study site — see §S1.1).
- **Power:** 10–57 VDC (wire terminal) or PoE; ≈1.5 W with all relays off.
- **Environmental / reliability:** operating range −40 to +75 °C; MTBF 111,199 h; FCC and CE certified.

A methodological consequence of the range mismatch is worth noting: at the wide native current range, the ±0.1 A accuracy of the commercial unit is coarse relative to the small currents drawn by an SHS, whereas the older purpose-built logger resolves current to the milliamp level. The adaptation described above [and the cleaning procedure in §S3] were applied to keep the commercial-unit measurements within an acceptable error band for the loads of interest.

## S3. Retained variables and processing

Although both loggers record a broader set of channels, the quantities retained for analysis were the **voltage and current of the photovoltaic module (generation)** and the **voltage and current of the loads (consumption)**. Instantaneous power was computed as the product of voltage and current on each side, and energy was integrated over the logging interval.

Because the two device types differ in native sampling and aggregation, and because per-household coverage varied from roughly one month to one year, the raw records were cleaned and resampled to a common temporal grid before analysis, ensuring that measurements from both logger types and across households were directly comparable.
