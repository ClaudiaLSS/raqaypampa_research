## Section S2: Energy Behavior Profiles (EBP) 

*This section of the Supplementary Material focus on the Energy Behavior profiles, from a detailed description to the classification tree used to assign each household with a profile in the paper.*

### S2.1 Profiles description

**Profile 1 — Educational & Agricultural Core.** Nuclear and stable-extended families with working-age adults and school-aged children; all heads are in agriculture or domestic labour, and 26 of 28 households have at least one child currently in school — the profile's defining structural feature. The daily routine centres on waking before dawn to prepare for fieldwork and school, an empty house through the working day, and a single dense evening of cooking, homework, and family time before an early bedtime. These households value schooling highly and ration energy strategically to guarantee power for children's evening study. Residence is sedentary: where the survey records temporary migration (9 of 28), it refers to one member's short-term labour absence while the household and its solar home system stay put, not a household relocation.

**Profile 2 — Isolated Elderly.** Households defined structurally by the absence of both school-aged dependents and a resident working-age support generation: unipersonal households and older couples living alone, whose adult children have out-migrated to Cochabamba, Chapare, Santa Cruz, or the mines. Household size is the smallest of any profile (mean 1.9), incomes the lowest (median 100 Bs), and 7 of 11 households contain a member aged 60 or older. The label is sociological rather than an age bracket — the classifier admits a few heads under 50 whose isolation is structural (siblings gone, no dependents) rather than geriatric. These households are almost entirely agricultural (10/11), sedentary year-round, and sustained by intermittent, unscheduled visits from migrated kin; their signature practice is using less electric devices.

**Profile 3 — Extended & Multi-Tasking Hub.** Extended and numerous families — 13 of 14 carry an *Extendido* or numerous family-type code — with the largest mean household size (6.4, range 3–11) and the highest median income of the four (300 Bs); most heads are agriculturalists (11/14). The label describes family *structure*, not co-residence: the classifier admits households that are extended on paper but frequently emptied in practice, so the profile is best read as large extended families with **intermittent occupancy** rather than a uniform high-intensity hub. What genuinely separates it from Profile 2 is later, longer evenings — driven by homework where school-age children are present — and real mobility, with households leaving for the *monte*, the mines, or a second residence, sometimes for months.

**Profile 4 — System Breakers.** Two overlapping populations rather than one uniform group. The profile is generated by the occupation override (Student and Mining heads, 7 households) together with structural residuals that resolve into no other category. It is the youngest profile (mean head age 38, median 31) and the most heterogeneous in family type. Its defining feature is not an appliance-level behaviour: when a household is present its routine is ordinary and describable, and the instability lives entirely in *whether* the household is home — through seasonal hardware transport, extended absence, or life split across two residences. This is also the thinnest-covered profile (50% interviewed), which matters because it carries the dual-residence and structural-absence claims that most depend on qualitative grounding.

### S2.2 Household classification tree

Every household within scope was assigned to one of the four Energy Behavior Profiles by a **fixed, priority-ordered decision tree** applied to survey-derived fields only. The tree is evaluated top to bottom and stops at the first matching rule. Qualitative material never assigns a profile directly; its only role in assignment is the documented, field-level correction of a miscoded input variable, after which the *same* tree is rerun. It is otherwise used downstream to characterize and validate the profiles.

**Scope**

The tree requires five fields, one of which (`portability_shs`) was collected only in the social-practices survey. The classified population is therefore the *N* = 65 households with complete coverage across both survey waves — a deliberate scope constraint, not incidental sample loss.

**Classifying variables and codes**

| Variable | Meaning | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| `occupation` | Main occupation of household head | 1 = Domestic labour · 2 = Agriculture · 3 = Student · 4 = Mining |
| `family_type` | Household composition | 1 = Unipersonal · 2 = Monoparental · 3 = Monoparental numeroso · 4 = Pareja nuclear · 5 = Nuclear completo · 6 = Nuclear completo numeroso · 7 = Extendido · 8 = Extendido sin hijos · 9 = Extendido numeroso · 10 = Extendido numeroso sin hijos |
| `children_in_school` | Family has children in school | 0 = No · 1 = Yes |
| `migration` | A family member migrates temporarily | 0 = No · 1 = Yes |
| `portability_shs` | Family keeps the SHS mostly at home | 0 = No · 1 = Yes |

Two derived groupings are used below:
- **Breaker occupations** = {Student (3), Mining (4)} — occupations that structurally decouple the household from the settlement's agricultural routine.
- **Extended/numerous family codes** = {Monoparental numeroso (3), Nuclear completo numeroso (6), Extendido (7), Extendido sin hijos (8), Extendido numeroso (9), Extendido numeroso sin hijos (10)}.

**The decision tree**

Evaluate in the following order; assign the profile at the first rule that matches and stop.

**Step 1 — Breaker-occupation override → Profile 4.**
If `occupation` ∈ {Student, Mining}, assign **Profile 4** regardless of family structure. This override captures the occupational actors of the Infrastructure Mobility, Dual-Home Displacement, and Hardware Resignation rules before any demographic test is applied.

**Step 2 — Solo / nuclear-couple → Profile 2.**
Else, if `family_type` ∈ {Unipersonal (1), Pareja nuclear (4)}, assign **Profile 2** (Isolated Elderly).

**Step 3 — Educational anchor (stable) → Profile 1.**
Else, assign **Profile 1** (Educational & Agricultural Core) if the household has a child in school (`children_in_school = Yes`) **and** one of the following holds:
- `family_type` is a nuclear code {Monoparental (2), Monoparental numeroso (3), Nuclear completo (5), Nuclear completo numeroso (6)}; **or**
- `family_type` is an extended code {Extendido (7), Extendido sin hijos (8), Extendido numeroso (9), Extendido numeroso sin hijos (10)} **and** the household is structurally stable, i.e. `migration = No` **and** `portability_shs = Yes`.

The stability sub-clause is what keeps a settled, multi-generational household organized around schooling in Profile 1 rather than defaulting to Profile 3 on family-type code alone. Every extended-family code must clear the same stability bar.

**Step 4 — Residual extended/numerous → Profile 3.**
Else, if `family_type` is an extended/numerous code, assign **Profile 3** (Extended & Multi-Tasking Hub). These are extended or numerous households that either have no child in school, or have one but failed the stability clause of Step 3 (active migration or a portable SHS).

**Step 5 — Residual → Profile 4.**
Else, assign **Profile 4** (System Breakers). This captures households whose family structure resolves cleanly into neither the solo/couple nor the extended/numerous categories (e.g. a non-numerous nuclear household with no child in school). Profile 4's population therefore combines two distinct mechanisms: the Step-1 occupational breakers and these Step-5 structural residuals.

**Reference implementation**

The tree is not re-implemented for this document. It is executed by `scripts/surveys/ebp_classification.py` in the study repository, which takes the coded survey table and writes the profile label for every household:

```
python scripts/surveys/ebp_classification.py \
    --input  data/clean/surveys/data_1.csv \
    --output results/energy_practices_surveys/user_classifications.csv \
    --sensitivity-log user_classifications_sensitivity_log.csv \
    --previous previous_classifications.csv \
    --audit-log user_classifications_audit_log.csv
```

**Missing-data handling**

Missing values (coded `-1`) are never allowed to pass silently through a comparison (which would treat "unknown" as a confirmed value). For any household with one or more missing required fields:

1. Enumerate every valid coded value for each missing field, per the codebook range.
2. Hold all known fields fixed and re-run the tree for every combination of plausible values.
3. If **all** combinations return the same profile, assign it — the missing field is not decisive for that household.
4. If combinations disagree, mark the household **"Unclassified — Insufficient Data"** and log which field(s) drove the ambiguity.

In this dataset, one household (id 65) had missing `family_type`, `children_in_school`, and `migration`; because its `occupation` was known to be a breaker (Student), every combination returned Profile 4, so it was assigned Profile 4 without ambiguity. No household within scope was left unclassified.

**Field-level corrections from qualitative evidence**

Where a household's assignment was flagged by the concordance check (§S2.2.8) or looked surprising, the interview and field memo were read against the *specific coded field* driving the assignment — not against the profile label. Where the qualitative record clearly contradicted a coded value, the field was corrected at source with a written justification, and the unchanged tree was rerun. Labels were never hand-assigned. Three households were examined; the counts in §S2.2.7 are reported after these corrections.

| id | Field(s) corrected | Old → new | Basis | Effect on profile |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 72 | `family_type` | 7 → 5 (Extendido → Nuclear completo) | Interview showed the co-resident household was nuclear; the two "extended" members were adult children who had moved away, not co-residing — failing the codebook's co-residence definition of *Extendido* | P3 → **P1** (changed) |
| 88 | `occupation`, `children_in_school` | (missing) → 2; 0 → 1 | `occupation` missing and `children_in_school = No` contradicted the transcript; both corrected from the researcher's field knowledge. Correcting only one left the household ambiguous, so both were fixed before the sensitivity check could resolve it | resolved to **P1** |
| 65 | none | — | Occupation known as Student (a breaker occupation), which alone determines the profile regardless of the three missing fields; resolved by the sensitivity check (§S2.2.5) with no correction | **P4** (no change) |

Only id 72's correction moved a household between profiles; id 88's corrections resolved an otherwise-unclassifiable household. Both effects are reflected in the final distribution below.

**Validation of the classification**

The finished classification is validated in two independent ways, neither of which edits the assignment:
- a **qualitative concordance check**, screening each classified household's interview and memo text for the thematic markers of its assigned profile and flagging disagreements for manual review; and
- a **quantitative discriminant-validity check**, confirming the groups separate along behavioral outcomes (e.g. daytime phone charging, agricultural labor hours, cooking duration, morning-lighting curve) that played no role in assignment.
